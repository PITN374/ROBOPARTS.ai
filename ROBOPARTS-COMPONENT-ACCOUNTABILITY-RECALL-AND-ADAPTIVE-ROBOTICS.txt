PURPOSE

ROBOPARTS provides a structured information framework for maintaining the identity, accountability, performance, compatibility, and lifecycle relationships of physical components used in robotic systems.

The framework extends beyond identifying a component or listing it in a marketplace.

Its purpose is to maintain meaningful relationships between the physical component and the information associated with that component throughout its existence.

These relationships may include:

Manufacturer. Supplier. Product. Part number. Revision. Lot. Serial number. Robot installation. Performance. Testing. Maintenance. Failure. Recall. Replacement. Lifecycle. Digital representation.

ROBOPARTS is intended to provide the physical-component intelligence layer of the broader PITN.ai robotics ecosystem.

==================================================

THE PERSISTENT IDENTITY PRINCIPLE

A physical component may move through multiple organizations and systems during its lifecycle.

The component may be:

Manufactured. Inspected. Distributed. Purchased. Installed. Operated. Serviced. Repaired. Transferred. Replaced. Retired.

Its identity should remain connected to its historical records throughout these transitions.

The proposed lifecycle relationship is:

MANUFACTURER | v PRODUCT | v PART NUMBER | v REVISION | v LOT / SERIAL | v PHYSICAL COMPONENT | v ROBOT INSTALLATION | v OPERATION | v MAINTENANCE | v REPLACEMENT | v RETIREMENT

The persistent identity should not be reassigned to a different physical component.

Replacement components should receive their own identities and retain a relationship to the component they replace.

==================================================

COMPONENT ACCOUNTABILITY

ROBOPARTS should support traceable relationships among the organizations responsible for different lifecycle activities.

MANUFACTURER:

Responsible for product identity and applicable manufacturing information.

SUPPLIER:

Responsible for distribution and applicable commercial records.

INTEGRATOR:

Responsible for incorporating components into a larger robotic system.

OPERATOR:

Responsible for authorized operation and operational records.

MAINTENANCE PROVIDER:

Responsible for service and maintenance records within its authority.

OWNER:

Responsible for applicable ownership and asset records.

These roles should be separately identifiable.

A participant should not automatically gain authority over another participant's records merely because it can view a component identity.

==================================================

RECALL AND SAFETY ACCOUNTABILITY

A major proposed function of ROBOPARTS is the ability to connect recall information and safety notices to affected physical components and the robots in which they are installed.

Consider a manufacturer identifying a defective actuator production lot.

The proposed relationship is:

MANUFACTURER | v PRODUCT MODEL | v HARDWARE REVISION | v PRODUCTION LOT | v SERIAL NUMBERS | v COMPONENT IDENTITIES | v INSTALLED ROBOTS | v OPERATORS / OWNERS / INTEGRATORS | v CORRECTIVE ACTION

A recall record may contain:

Recall identifier. Issuing organization. Affected product. Affected revision. Affected lot. Affected serial numbers. Description of issue. Risk classification. Issue date. Applicable corrective action. Replacement requirements. Verification requirements. Status. Supporting documentation.

The system should preserve the source, authority, and date associated with each recall record.

==================================================

KILL STATUS AND OPERATIONAL RESTRICTIONS

ROBOPARTS proposes a .killstatus namespace for identity-linked restriction and safety-status information.

The purpose is to provide a structured reference to component restrictions and operational status.

Proposed status values:

NORMAL:

No recorded restriction.

NOTICE:

An advisory or service notice exists.

INVESTIGATION:

The component is under technical investigation.

RESTRICTED:

Operation is subject to defined restrictions.

RECALL:

The component is identified in an applicable recall.

QUARANTINED:

The component has been isolated pending investigation or corrective action.

VERIFIED:

Applicable corrective action has been completed and verified.

RETIRED:

The component has been removed from active service.

Status records should identify:

Issuing authority. Reason. Affected identity. Effective date. Evidence. Applicable restrictions. Authorization. Status history. Resolution.

IMPORTANT SAFETY DISTINCTION:

ROBOPARTS provides identity-linked accountability and status information.

A blockchain record or namespace does not independently constitute a safety-rated control mechanism.

Actual physical disabling or restriction of a robot must be implemented through authorized, validated safety systems and appropriate hardware and software controls.

Emergency stops, safety controllers, certified interlocks, and human override mechanisms must remain independently effective.

COMPONENT PERFORMANCE HISTORY

A component's lifecycle information may include operational performance over time.

For an actuator, relevant records may include:

Operating hours. Cycle count. Average load. Peak load. Temperature. Current. Torque. Speed. Vibration. Maintenance events. Failure events. Performance trends. Inspection results.

For a battery, relevant records may include:

Charge cycles. Capacity. Temperature. Voltage. State of health. Fault events. Maintenance. Replacement history.

For a sensor, relevant records may include:

Calibration. Accuracy. Drift. Operating hours. Environmental exposure. Failure events.

The information structure should allow component-specific performance profiles.

ADAPTIVE MAINTENANCE

ROBOPARTS can provide the component identity relationships needed to connect operational information with maintenance and replacement decisions.

The proposed workflow is:

OPERATIONAL DATA | v COMPONENT PERFORMANCE | v TREND ANALYSIS | v IDENTIFIED DEGRADATION | v MAINTENANCE REQUIREMENT | v REQUIRED REPLACEMENT PART | v ROBOPARTS | v COMPATIBLE COMPONENT | v SERVICE / REPLACEMENT

Potential applications include:

Preventive maintenance. Condition-based maintenance. Failure analysis. Replacement identification. Service planning. Inventory planning. Component lifecycle forecasting.

Predictions should be distinguished from confirmed failures and verified maintenance requirements.

==================================================

HARDWARE-AWARE ROBOT CAPABILITIES

A robot's capabilities depend partly on the physical components currently installed and available.

A capability should not automatically be considered available merely because it appears in an original product description.

For example:

A robot may have a robotic hand.

The hand may subsequently be removed, disabled, damaged, or replaced.

Its current manipulation capability may therefore differ from its original declared capability.

ROBOPARTS should support the relationship:

ROBOT IDENTITY | v CURRENT HARDWARE | v COMPONENT STATUS | v CAPABILITY REQUIREMENTS | v TESTING | v VERIFIED CAPABILITY | v TASK ELIGIBILITY

This distinction enables a digital representation to reflect the current physical configuration rather than relying exclusively on an original specification.

==================================================

DECLARED AND VERIFIED CAPABILITIES

ROBOPARTS should distinguish three capability conditions.

DECLARED:

A capability is specified by a manufacturer, integrator, or authorized source.

TESTED:

A capability has undergone a defined test procedure.

VERIFIED:

The capability has satisfied identified acceptance criteria under documented conditions.

A verification record may contain:

Robot identity. Component identity. Capability. Test procedure. Test date. Environment. Performance result. Acceptance criteria. Verification authority. Verification status.

A capability should also be capable of becoming:

EXPIRED. SUSPENDED. REVOKED. REQUIRES RETESTING.

This allows a robot's capability profile to evolve when its hardware, configuration, or operating condition changes.

ADAPTIVE ROBOTICS AND LEARNING

The broader PITN ecosystem recognizes that robotic systems may adapt through:

Software updates. AI updates. Configuration changes. User preferences. Environmental learning. Task experience. Hardware upgrades. Component replacement. Maintenance. Performance feedback.

ROBOPARTS provides the physical hardware relationship layer associated with these changes.

A simplified adaptive loop is:

TASK | v OBSERVATION | v DECISION | v ACTION | v RESULT | v FEEDBACK | v EVALUATION | v ADJUSTMENT | v FUTURE BEHAVIOR

An adjustment may involve software, configuration, memory, user preference, or authorized hardware modification.

RELATIONSHIP TO ROBOTRAITS.AI

RoboTraits.ai and RoboTraits.com are complementary intellectual-property components within the PITN ecosystem.

RoboTraits focuses on the descriptive and behavioral profile of a robotic agent.

Its conceptual information may include:

Core characteristics. Behavioral attributes. Configured personality. Learned preferences. Interaction preferences. Contextual behavior.

The distinction between core personality and learned preferences should be maintained.

For example:

CORE PERSONALITY:

Patient.

LEARNED PREFERENCE:

A particular user prefers slower conversation.

CONTEXT:

Personal assistant interaction.

RESULT:

A slower conversational response.

A learned preference should not automatically rewrite the underlying personality configuration.

ROBOPARTS contributes information about the physical capabilities and limitations that constrain available behaviors.

==================================================

RELATIONSHIP TO ROBOAGENTIC.AI

RoboAgentic.ai focuses on intelligent-agent implementation.

It may consume relevant information from ROBOPARTS and RoboTraits.

The conceptual relationship is:

ROBOPARTS | v PHYSICAL HARDWARE | v VERIFIED CAPABILITIES | v ROBOTRAITS | v CHARACTERISTICS / BEHAVIORAL PROFILE | v ROBOAGENTIC | v INTELLIGENT AGENT | v AUTHORIZED ROBOT ACTION

The agent should understand:

Current hardware. Available capabilities. Disabled components. Physical constraints. Operating limits. Safety restrictions. Verified task eligibility.

An intelligent agent must not be permitted to bypass applicable physical, electrical, mechanical, or operational safety constraints.

HARDWARE UPGRADE AND CAPABILITY EXPANSION

A hardware upgrade can change the capabilities available to a robotic system.

Example:

EXISTING ROBOT | v IDENTIFY LIMITATION | v REQUIRED CAPABILITY | v REQUIRED COMPONENT | v ROBOPARTS SEARCH | v COMPATIBILITY ASSESSMENT | v COMPONENT PROCUREMENT | v INSTALLATION | v CONFIGURATION UPDATE | v TESTING | v CAPABILITY VERIFICATION | v UPDATED ROBOT PROFILE

The original robot identity remains persistent.

The installed hardware identity changes where appropriate.

The robot's configuration history records the transition.

==================================================

CONFIGURATION AND LEARNING HISTORY

A robotic system may maintain multiple configuration layers.

PHYSICAL CONFIGURATION. SOFTWARE CONFIGURATION. AI CONFIGURATION. PERSONALITY CONFIGURATION. USER PREFERENCES. CURRENT CONTEXT.

These layers should be separately identifiable.

A configuration history may include:

Configuration 1.0. Software update. Configuration 1.1. Hardware upgrade. Configuration 2.0. Behavioral configuration update. Configuration 2.1.

Learning history should remain distinguishable from immutable identity information.

Changes should be attributable to an authorized source and recorded with applicable timestamps, revisions, and evidence.

==================================================

PHYSICAL SAFETY CONSTRAINTS

Adaptive robotics must operate within applicable physical and safety constraints.

Examples include:

Force limits. Speed limits. Payload limits. Joint limits. Temperature limits. Battery limits. Operating environment. Restricted areas. Emergency stop. Human override.

Learning systems and intelligent agents must not be permitted to bypass these constraints.

Safety-critical restrictions must be enforced through appropriate validated hardware and software architectures.

ROBOPARTS can provide identity, component status, and associated constraint information.

It is not a replacement for certified safety engineering or safety-rated control systems.

==================================================

DIGITAL TWINS AND COMPONENT IDENTITY

A digital twin or digital representation can incorporate the identity and relationships of the physical components that make up a robot.

The proposed relationship is:

PHYSICAL ROBOT | v DIGITAL REPRESENTATION | v ROBOT IDENTITY | v COMPONENT IDENTITIES | v CONFIGURATION | v PERFORMANCE | v MAINTENANCE | v LEARNING HISTORY | v LIFECYCLE

Multiple robots may maintain individual digital representations within a broader multi-robot environment.

This can support comparisons involving:

Hardware. Capabilities. Configuration. Performance. Tasks. Maintenance. Lifecycle.

The digital representation should reflect documented changes to the physical system.

==================================================

MULTI-ROBOT KNOWLEDGE AND CAPABILITY TRANSFER

A future PITN implementation may support relationships between compatible robot platforms.

For example:

ROBOT A | v LEARNING / PERFORMANCE | v KNOWLEDGE OR MODEL | v COMPATIBILITY ASSESSMENT | v ROBOT B | v ADAPTATION | v TESTING | v VERIFICATION

Transfer must account for differences in:

Hardware. Sensors. Actuators. Control systems. Physical dimensions. Capabilities. Safety constraints. Operating environments.

Knowledge transfer does not automatically establish equivalent physical capability.

The receiving robot must undergo applicable adaptation and verification.

==================================================

MULTI-ROBOT APPLICATIONS

ROBOPARTS can provide a common component and capability information structure across multiple robotic systems.

Potential applications include:

Robot matching. Task assignment. Component sourcing. Robot coordination. Fleet maintenance. Digital twins. Lifecycle management. Human-robot interaction. Hardware compatibility. Service and replacement.

The conceptual relationship is:

TASK REQUIREMENTS | v ROBOT CAPABILITIES | v ROBOT IDENTITIES | v ROBOPARTS COMPONENT DATA | v CURRENT HARDWARE | v PERFORMANCE HISTORY | v CURRENT OPERATING STATE | v ROBOT MATCH

Actual coordination and task allocation depend on application-specific software, communication, authorization, and validated implementation.

==================================================

DATA GOVERNANCE AND ACCOUNTABILITY

Persistent component identity requires reliable data governance.

The proposed architecture should support:

Identity ownership. Record authorship. Source attribution. Revision history. Access permissions. Verification status. Digital signatures. Audit trails. Record corrections. Dispute resolution. Data retention.

A distinction should be maintained between:

Immutable identity references. Mutable product information. Historical lifecycle events. Current operating status. Verified performance. Unverified claims.

Where a record is corrected, the original historical record and the correction relationship should remain auditable.

Public identity does not require public disclosure of proprietary engineering specifications, confidential manufacturing data, personal information, or commercially sensitive records.

COMMERCIAL AND INDUSTRY APPLICATION

The combination of persistent component identity, compatibility, performance, lifecycle, and accountability can support a broader robotics information and commercial ecosystem.

Potential workflows include:

The commercial opportunity is to connect information that is currently distributed across manufacturers, suppliers, robot OEMs, integrators, operators, and service organizations.

The intended relationship is:

MANUFACTURER | v COMPONENT | v PERSISTENT IDENTITY | v ROBOT / BOM | v CUSTOMER | v OPERATION | v SERVICE | v REPLACEMENT | v LIFECYCLE
