ROBOPARTS Core Concept
Overview

ROBOPARTS is a PITN project focused on organizing and connecting information about robotic parts, robotic capabilities, tasks, digital twins, robot identity, lifecycle information, and robotics applications.

The concept is intended to provide a structured way to describe relationships between robotic components and the larger robotic systems in which those components operate.

ROBOPARTS can therefore be understood as an information and intelligence framework surrounding robotic parts and their relationship to robotic systems.

From Parts to Robotic Systems

A robotic system is more than an individual physical component.

Robots can contain and depend upon many types of components, including mechanical, electrical, sensing, control, power, motion, and other subsystem elements. Understanding those components in context can help connect information about a part with the capabilities and functions of the larger robotic system.

The public ROBOPARTS documentation addresses this broader relationship between components, capabilities, tasks, and robotic systems.

Capabilities and Performance

ROBOPARTS documentation includes concepts for describing the performance and capabilities associated with robotic components and systems.

This provides a foundation for relating information about a component to what a robotic system can do.

Examples of information that may be relevant include:

component characteristics
performance information
capabilities
functions
tasks
relationships between components and systems

The purpose is to make this information more structured and useful across the robotics ecosystem.

Tasks and Robot Matching

The public ROBOPARTS material also addresses task ontology and robot matching.

A task represents something a robotic system is intended to accomplish. A robot or robotic system has capabilities that may make it suitable for particular tasks.

Organizing this information creates a basis for relating:

parts → capabilities → robots → tasks

This relationship is an important part of the ROBOPARTS concept.

Digital Twins and Lifecycle Information

ROBOPARTS documentation also addresses digital twins and robot lifecycle information.

Digital representations can provide a way to associate information about a physical robotic system with information about its components, capabilities, identity, operation, and lifecycle.

This creates a broader information context in which robotic parts can be understood as components of evolving robotic systems rather than as isolated objects.

Robot Identity

The public ROBOPARTS documentation includes concepts relating to digital identity and robotic lifecycle information.

Identity can provide a structured reference for associating information with a particular robotic system, its components, capabilities, and lifecycle.

This can support continuity of information as robotic systems are developed, deployed, modified, maintained, or otherwise managed throughout their lifecycle.

Broader Robotics Ecosystem

ROBOPARTS is documented as part of a broader robotics ecosystem that can include:

robotic components
robotic systems
robot capabilities
tasks
digital twins
applications
intelligent systems
human-robot interaction
multi-robot environments

The purpose of this broader view is to connect information that would otherwise remain separated across different parts of the robotics lifecycle.
