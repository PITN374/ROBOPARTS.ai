27-NAME PHYSICAL BOM NAMESPACE ARCHITECTURE

Version: 1.0.0 Author: Aaron Bailot Founder: PITN — Power In The Numbers Project: ROBOPARTS.ai Parent Ecosystem: PITN.ai Document Type: Foundational Architecture Specification

============================================================

PURPOSE

This document establishes the proposed 27-name Physical Bill of Materials (BOM) Namespace Architecture for ROBOPARTS.ai.

ROBOPARTS provides a structured framework for identifying, classifying, organizing, connecting, and managing the physical components and assemblies that make up robotic systems.

The architecture establishes standardized namespace classifications for the major physical elements of a robot, from the complete robotic system down to individual components and interconnects.

Each namespace provides a designated classification entry point for associating physical components with their manufacturer information, product identity, part numbers, revisions, BOM positions, lot numbers, serial numbers, physical installations, and lifecycle records.

The objective is to connect physical robotics hardware to persistent component identity and the information systems that describe it.

============================================================

THE 27-NAME PHYSICAL BOM ARCHITECTURE

The following table establishes the proposed 27 namespaces and their primary manufacturer BOM functions.

SYSTEM Namespace: .robotsystem Function: Top-level robot system and physical BOM boundary.
ASSEMBLY Namespace: .roboticarm Function: Robotic arm assembly.
ASSEMBLY Namespace: .roboticleg Function: Robotic leg assembly.
ASSEMBLY Namespace: .robotichand Function: Robotic hand assembly.
ASSEMBLY Namespace: .roboticwrist Function: Robotic wrist and associated subassemblies.
MOTION Namespace: .actuator Function: Motors, servos, rotary actuators, linear actuators, and joint drives.
MOTION Namespace: .reducer Function: Gearboxes, reduction mechanisms, and mechanical transmission systems.
MOTION Namespace: .mechanicalinterface Function: Flanges, mounts, couplers, adapters, and mechanical connection interfaces.
MOTION Namespace: .encoder Function: Position, velocity, and rotational feedback components.
MECHANICAL Namespace: .fastener Function: Bolts, screws, nuts, washers, and mechanical fastening hardware.
SENSING Namespace: .robotsensor Function: General robotic sensors and sensing devices.
SENSING Namespace: .sensingmodule Function: Integrated sensing assemblies and sensor modules.
SENSING Namespace: .torquesensor Function: Torque measurement and monitoring components.
SENSING Namespace: .forcesensor Function: Force and load measurement components.
SENSING Namespace: .forcetorque Function: Combined force and torque measurement systems.
SAFETY Namespace: .safetysensor Function: Safety, presence, contact, and protective sensing components.
CONTROL Namespace: .controlsystem Function: Robotic control hardware and control-system assemblies.
CONTROL Namespace: .robotcontroller Function: Robot controllers, computing hardware, and input/output systems.
ENERGY Namespace: .batterymodule Function: Battery assemblies and integrated battery modules.
ENERGY Namespace: .batterycell Function: Individual battery cells.
ENERGY Namespace: .powerconverter Function: Power conversion electronics and electrical power management components.
VISION Namespace: .visionmodule Function: Integrated robotic vision assemblies.
VISION Namespace: .visionsensor Function: Vision sensing hardware.
VISION Namespace: .cameramodule Function: Camera, depth-camera, stereo-camera, and imaging assemblies.
INTERACTION Namespace: .endeffector Function: End effectors, robotic tools, grippers, and task-specific interaction hardware.
INTERACTION Namespace: .toolchanger Function: Robotic tool-changing mechanisms and associated assemblies.
INTERCONNECT Namespace: .cableharness Function: Electrical wiring, communication cables, and physical cable harness assemblies.

FUNCTIONAL ARCHITECTURE

The 27 namespaces operate within a hierarchical physical BOM structure.

                     .robotsystem
                          |
         +----------------+----------------+
         |                |                |
   .roboticarm      .roboticleg      .robotichand
         |                |                |
         +-------- .roboticwrist -----------+
                          |
                   PHYSICAL BOM
                          |
   +--------------+-------+--------+-------------+
   |              |       |        |             |
 MOTION         SENSING CONTROL   ENERGY        VISION
   |              |       |        |             |


.actuator .robotsensor .controlsystem .batterymodule .visionmodule .reducer .sensingmodule .robotcontroller .batterycell .visionsensor .mechanicalinterface .torquesensor .powerconverter .cameramodule .encoder .forcesensor .fastener .forcetorque .safetysensor | +---------------- INTERACTION | | | .endeffector | .toolchanger | +---------------- INTERCONNECT | .cableharness

The physical BOM establishes the relationships between complete robotic systems, assemblies, functional subsystems, and individual physical components.

A component may belong to more than one functional relationship depending on its physical installation, design, and application.

The architecture is intended to support hierarchical BOM relationships without restricting components to a single operational role.

SEVEN BROWSER CATEGORIES

For public ROBOPARTS browsing and component discovery, the 27 namespaces are organized into seven primary browser categories.

CATEGORY 1: CORE SYSTEM

.robotssystem .roboticarm .roboticleg .robotichand .roboticwrist .fastener .cableharness

Primary function: Physical robot structure, major assemblies, fastening hardware, and interconnects.

CATEGORY 2: ACTUATORS

.actuator .reducer .mechanicalinterface .encoder

Primary function: Movement, torque generation, mechanical transmission, joint interfaces, and motion feedback.

CATEGORY 3: SENSORS

.robotsensor .sensingmodule .torquesensor .forcesensor .forcetorque .safetysensor

Primary function: Measurement, perception-related sensing, force feedback, and safety-related detection.

CATEGORY 4: CONTROLLERS

.controlsystem .robotcontroller

Primary function: Control hardware, computing, command execution, and input/output infrastructure.

CATEGORY 5: POWER SYSTEMS

.batterymodule .batterycell .powerconverter

Primary function: Energy storage, battery assemblies, electrical power conversion, and distribution.

CATEGORY 6: VISION MODULES

.visionmodule .visionsensor .cameramodule

Primary function: Imaging, visual perception hardware, camera assemblies, and vision infrastructure.

CATEGORY 7: GRIPPERS

.endeffector .toolchanger

Primary function: Physical interaction, robotic manipulation, task-specific tools, and end-effector exchange.

The seven browser categories provide a simplified discovery interface while retaining the underlying 27-name physical BOM classification architecture.

NOTE:

The browser categories are a presentation and discovery structure.

The 27 namespaces are the proposed physical component classification structure.

These two levels should remain separately identifiable.

ARCHITECTURAL LOGIC

The seven browser categories correspond to the major physical functions of a robotic machine.

CORE SYSTEM: Structure, assemblies, physical organization, and interconnect.

ACTUATORS: Movement, mechanical force, torque, and motion transmission.

SENSORS: Measurement, feedback, force detection, and safety-related sensing.

CONTROLLERS: Computation, control hardware, and command infrastructure.

POWER SYSTEMS: Energy storage, conversion, and electrical distribution.

VISION MODULES: Visual sensing, imaging, and perception hardware.

GRIPPERS: Physical interaction, manipulation, and task-specific tooling.

The overall functional relationship can be represented as:

              PHYSICAL MACHINE
                     |
         +-----------+-----------+
         |           |           |
       POWER       CONTROL     SENSING
         |           |           |
         +-----------+-----------+
                     |
                   MOTION
                     |
                   VISION
                     |
                INTERACTION
                     |
                PHYSICAL WORK


This is a conceptual functional relationship, not a mandatory sequence of physical operation.

The actual architecture and control dependencies depend on the robot's design and application.

CORE MANUFACTURER IDENTITY CHAIN

Every namespace should support relationships to the underlying manufacturer and physical component records.

The proposed manufacturer identity chain is:

NAMESPACE | v ASSEMBLY / COMPONENT | v PART | v MANUFACTURER | v MANUFACTURER PART NUMBER (MPN) | v DISTRIBUTOR SKU | v BOM POSITION | v REVISION / CONFIGURATION | v MANUFACTURING LOT / SERIAL | v PHYSICAL ASSET | v TEST / CALIBRATION | v INSTALLATION | v SERVICE / LIFECYCLE | v PERSISTENT DIGITAL IDENTITY

This chain provides a conceptual foundation for connecting manufacturer records, commercial identifiers, engineering information, and individual physical assets.

The manufacturer's original part number, distributor SKU, engineering revision, and physical serial number should remain preserved as distinct source identifiers.

A ROBOPARTS/PITN identity is intended to connect these identifiers rather than replace them.

============================================================

PERSISTENT COMPONENT IDENTITY

The namespace is the classification entry point.

Persistent component identity is the infrastructure.

The relationships are where the value accumulates.

A component identity may connect:

Namespace classification
Component designation
Manufacturer identity
Manufacturer part number
Product model
Distributor SKU
Engineering revision
Manufacturing lot
Individual serial number
Physical installation
Robot and subsystem association
Testing and calibration
Service and maintenance
Replacement and lifecycle history

The persistent identity concept is intended to maintain continuity of reference even when commercial identifiers, ownership, sourcing relationships, or installation locations change.

Replacement, refurbishment, and revision changes should be recorded through traceable relationships rather than silently overwriting historical records.

The implementation must distinguish between a product or part designation and an individual serialized physical component.

COMPONENT ACCOUNTABILITY, TRACEABILITY AND RECALL

ROBOPARTS is intended to support traceable relationships between component identity, manufacturer, product revision, manufacturing lot, serial number, installation history, and affected robotic systems.

These relationships can provide a foundation for:

Identifying potentially affected components.
Tracing component installation locations.
Identifying robotic systems containing affected components.
Recording maintenance and corrective actions.
Coordinating authorized replacement.
Maintaining service and lifecycle records.
Supporting recall investigations.
Preserving component accountability.

Future implementations may incorporate component safety status, recall notifications, authorization controls, and restrictions on continued use.

Safety-critical disablement or kill-switch functionality must be implemented through validated control systems and authorized safety mechanisms.

A namespace or digital identity alone does not physically disable a component.

WEB3 NAMESPACE AND ADDRESSING DIRECTION

The 27-name architecture establishes proposed human-readable namespace classifications for physical robotics components and assemblies.

Examples include:

.actuator .encoder .endeffector .robotsystem .robotcontroller .batterymodule .visionmodule

These names may provide classification and addressing entry points within a future interoperable digital identity architecture.

Potential applications include:

Component identity resolution.
Digital passport references.
Manufacturer and product relationships.
Cross-platform component identification.
Lifecycle record association.
Digital twin relationships.
Authorized identity verification.
Web2-to-Web3 identity interoperability.

The namespaces documented here are proposed architectural classifications.

Their publication does not, by itself, establish registration as decentralized identifiers, blockchain assets, or functioning Web3 domain names.

Actual registration, resolution, ownership, authorization, and interoperability require defined technical protocols and implementation.

IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLES

The proposed 27-name architecture is intended to follow these principles:

Preserve manufacturer source identifiers.
Distinguish product identity from individual physical identity.
Maintain traceable BOM relationships.
Support hierarchical assemblies and component relationships.
Preserve revision and configuration history.
Connect components to their physical robotic installations.
Support component compatibility and capability information.
Maintain lifecycle and service relationships.
Distinguish declared specifications from tested and verified performance.
Support future interoperability with existing ERP, PLM, MES, CAD, procurement, quality, and service systems.
Support future digital identity and Web3 addressing implementations.
Maintain appropriate authorization and safety boundaries.

The architecture is intended to complement existing enterprise systems rather than require their replacement.

============================================================

FOUNDATIONAL ARCHITECTURAL PROPOSITION

A robotic system is not simply one machine.

It is an interconnected collection of physical components, assemblies, subsystems, and supporting infrastructure.

Each component may have its own manufacturer, commercial identity, technical specifications, installation requirements, performance characteristics, and lifecycle.

ROBOPARTS establishes a proposed 27-name Physical BOM Namespace Architecture to organize these relationships.

The objective is to connect the physical component to the information that describes it and preserve those relationships throughout its lifecycle.

From an individual actuator to a complete humanoid robot, the same architectural principle applies.

IDENTIFY THE COMPONENT. CONNECT ITS RELATIONSHIPS. UNDERSTAND ITS CAPABILITIES. PRESERVE ITS HISTORY. MAINTAIN ITS ACCOUNTABILITY.
