ROBOPARTS.ai — LIFECYCLE AND TRACEABILITY

Document ID: RP-LIFECYCLE-009
Version: 1.0
Status: Proposed Lifecycle Architecture
Parent Project: PITN.ai (Power in the Numbers)
Author: Aaron Bailot
Development Stage: Stage 1

1. PURPOSE

This document defines the proposed lifecycle and traceability architecture for RoboParts.ai.

The objective is to maintain a structured record of robotic components from initial sourcing through installation, operation, maintenance, replacement, recall, and retirement.

RoboParts.ai is intended to connect physical component identity with robot configuration, supplier accountability, maintenance history, and future capability changes.

2. LIFECYCLE MODEL

Proposed component lifecycle:

REQUIREMENT | v PRODUCT SELECTION | v SUPPLIER SOURCING | v RFQ | v SAMPLE | v SAMPLE EVALUATION | v ACCEPTANCE | v PILOT ORDER | v RECEIVING INSPECTION | v COMPONENT REGISTRATION | v ROBOT INSTALLATION | v OPERATION | v MAINTENANCE | v REPLACEMENT OR RETIREMENT

Each stage should produce traceable records.

5. SAMPLE QUALIFICATION

Sample evaluation should follow a controlled process.

5.1 Fit Baseline

Record:

Joint position.
Robot class.
Available outer diameter.
Available axial length.
Hollow-shaft requirement.
Mounting interface.
Cable exit.
Initial quantity.

Acceptance objective:

At least one candidate model must map to the mechanical envelope without inventing unconfirmed dimensions.

5.2 Duty and Thermal Check

Record:

Continuous output.
Peak output.
Peak duration.
Speed or stroke.
Operating cycle.
Current limit.
Ambient temperature.
Cooling assumptions.

Acceptance objective:

Selection must be based on the expected operating cycle, not peak ratings alone.

5.3 Interface and Control Check

Record:

Bus voltage.
Communication protocol.
Controller location.
Encoder.
Brake.
Pinout.
Cable length.
Firmware integration boundary.

Acceptance objective:

Electrical and control interfaces must be sufficiently clear for the intended bench test.

5.4 Sample Validation

Define the actual pass/fail test before sample acceptance.

Potential tests:

Backlash.
Thermal rise.
Noise.
Force response.
Impact response.
Stroke repeatability.
Position accuracy.
Current consumption.
Communication stability.

Each test should reference the applicable test procedure and revision.

5.5 Pilot Continuity

Record:

Pilot quantity.
Repeat-order forecast.
Inspection requirements.
Packaging.
Shipping restrictions.
Incoterm.
Delivery destination.
Revision control.
Change notification requirements.

An accepted sample is not automatically proof of repeat-order or production readiness.

6. RECEIVING INSPECTION

A received component should be compared against the purchase and engineering baseline.

Suggested inspection information:

Purchase order.
Manufacturer.
Model.
Part number.
Serial number.
Lot number.
Product revision.
Drawing revision.
Firmware version.
Accessories received.
Packaging condition.
Visible damage.
Documentation received.
Inspection outcome.

The inspection should identify discrepancies between the ordered configuration and the received product.

9. REPLACEMENT TRACEABILITY

When a component is replaced, the system should preserve both the removed and replacement component identities.

Example:

ROBOT-0001 | +-- Original Actuator: RP-000001 | +-- Removal Event: EVT-000101 | +-- Replacement Actuator: RP-000025

The replacement record should include:

Removed component ID.
Replacement component ID.
Robot ID.
Subsystem.
Removal date.
Installation date.
Reason for replacement.
Replacement authorization.
Compatibility assessment.
Test results.
Updated BOM revision.

The historical record must not simply overwrite the original component assignment.

10. RECALL MANAGEMENT

RoboParts.ai should support structured recall and safety-notice management.

A recall record may contain:

recall_id
manufacturer_id
product_id
affected_model
affected_revision
affected_serial_range
affected_lot
recall_reference
publication_date
severity
required_action
status
evidence_reference

The system should identify robots and assemblies containing affected components.

Potential responses:

Inspection.
Restricted use.
Quarantine.
Component replacement.
Firmware update.
Manufacturer return.
Retirement.

A recall notice must be distinguished from a confirmed defect in an individual unit.

11. DIGITAL ACCOUNTABILITY

The architecture should allow a component's history to be reconstructed.

A component record may connect:

MANUFACTURER | v PRODUCT MODEL | v SERIALIZED COMPONENT | v SUPPLIER | v PURCHASE | v INSPECTION | v ROBOT INSTALLATION | v OPERATING HISTORY | v MAINTENANCE | v REPLACEMENT OR RETIREMENT

This provides a foundation for component accountability across the robotics lifecycle.

12. ADAPTIVE MAINTENANCE

Operational data may help identify maintenance requirements.

Potential indicators:

Increasing operating temperature.
Higher current consumption.
Increased vibration.
Position error.
Backlash growth.
Increased failure frequency.
Reduced battery performance.
Changes in cycle time.

Proposed process:

OPERATIONAL DATA | v COMPONENT ANALYSIS | v PERFORMANCE TREND | v MAINTENANCE ASSESSMENT | v REQUIRED PART | v ROBOPARTS.AI | v MAINTENANCE ACTION

Predictive maintenance recommendations must be distinguished from confirmed failure diagnoses.

13. HARDWARE CHANGES AND CAPABILITY HISTORY

Hardware replacement or upgrades can change a robot's capabilities.

Examples:

New robotic hand.
Higher-performance actuator.
Additional sensor.
New controller.
Battery replacement.
End-effector change.
Updated communication hardware.

The system should record:

Previous hardware.
New hardware.
Installation event.
Updated configuration.
Required testing.
Capability reassessment.
Verification status.

A hardware upgrade must not automatically create a verified capability.

14. DIGITAL TWIN AND LIFECYCLE

A digital twin may maintain a historical representation of:

Maintenance.

The digital representation should identify when information was last synchronized and whether it reflects verified physical state.

15. END-OF-LIFE AND RETIREMENT

A component or robot may reach end of life through:

Wear.
Damage.
Manufacturer discontinuation.
Safety concerns.
Economic obsolescence.
Incompatibility.
Recall.
Replacement.
Retirement of the complete robot.

Retirement records should include:

Retirement identifier.
Component or robot ID.
Retirement date.
Reason.
Final status.
Disposition.
Data retention reference.
Related replacement records.

Retired identities should remain historically traceable.

16. DATA RETENTION AND AUDITABILITY

The proposed system should preserve:

Identity records.
Installation history.
BOM revisions.
Inspection records.
Maintenance records.
Fault history.
Recall history.
Replacement events.
Retirement records.

Corrections should be attributable and timestamped.

Historical records should not be silently rewritten to conceal previous configurations or events.

Access, retention, privacy, and deletion policies must be established for the actual deployment.
