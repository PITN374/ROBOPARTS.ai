ROBOPARTS.ai — CATALOG AND PRODUCT INDEX

Document ID: RP-CATALOG-001
Version: 1.0
Status: Proposed Catalog Architecture
Parent Project: PITN.ai
Development Stage: Stage 1

1. Purpose

Define a structured, searchable catalog of robotics components and associated product families.

The catalog must support early architecture selection, engineering comparison, supplier sourcing, RFQ preparation, sample evaluation, and eventual lifecycle traceability.

The catalog must distinguish:

Product families.
Manufacturer model numbers.
Individual commercial products.
Product revisions.
Physical component instances.
Custom OEM variants.
Accessories and associated controllers.
2. Catalog Classification Structure

Proposed hierarchy:

ROBOTICS COMPONENTS

Actuators and motion systems.
Motors.
Reducers and gearboxes.
Linear motion systems.
Sensors.
Controllers and electronics.
Robotic hands and end effectors.
Structural and mechanical components.
Wiring and connectivity.
Batteries and power systems.
Integrated robotic assemblies.

Each classification must have a stable internal identifier and a versioned taxonomy.

4. Humanoid Joint Classification Index

The first selection matrix should cover:

Joint	Primary requirements	Additional review
Hip	Torque, speed, duty	Impact, thermal, bearing loads
Knee	Torque, speed, acceleration	Overload, stiffness, thermal
Ankle	Torque, speed, control	Shock, compactness, stiffness
Shoulder	Torque, speed, envelope	Mass, routing, mirrored fit
Elbow	Torque, speed, mass	Backlash, cable routing
Wrist	Compactness, precision	Bore, connector, stiffness
Neck	Torque, speed, noise	Safety, range, mechanism
Hand	Force, speed, precision	Transmission, tactile integration

This is a coverage framework, not a declaration that one actuator architecture fits every joint.

5. Joint-Level Engineering Requirements

Each joint position must have an independently defined requirement record.

Required fields:

Joint position.
Number of axes.
Required continuous torque.
Required peak torque.
Peak torque duration.
Output speed.
Acceleration and deceleration profile.
Operating cycle.
Expected load.
Robot mass and payload assumptions.
Ambient temperature.
Cooling conditions.
Mechanical envelope.
Mounting interface.
Electrical requirements.
Feedback requirements.
Control architecture.
Communication protocol.

Torque, speed, force, and stroke must use explicit units.

Continuous and peak ratings must never be merged into one specification.

10. Product Selection Workflow
Define robot architecture and joint map.
Define torque and speed requirements by joint.
Establish mechanical envelope.
Define electrical and control assumptions.
Filter applicable actuator architectures.
Compare standard catalog products.
Review performance and thermal evidence.
Identify missing information.
Shortlist candidate models.
Request controlled engineering documentation.
Evaluate sample readiness.
Complete sample acceptance.
Consider OEM modifications only after baseline fit is established.
11. Product-Family Selection Rules

Do not compare unrelated architectures using one undifferentiated torque ranking.

Separate:

Integrated rotary modules.
QDD modules.
Frameless motors.
Linear actuators.
Controllers.
Reducers.
Accessories.

A catalog-level match is a preliminary engineering candidate, not proof of application suitability.

12. Initial Catalog Coverage Target

The first catalog should prioritize:

Humanoid robot prototype platforms.
Bipedal leg and arm development.
University and robotics laboratory sourcing.
Standard actuator models.
Sample and pilot-order readiness.

Broad torque-class coverage is useful during early architecture sizing, but the final selection must be tied to actual joint requirements.

13. Catalog Evidence Policy

Public catalog pages may contain:

Product family introductions.
Reference images.
General model information.
Public selection specifications.
Application descriptions.

Do not present fabricated factory photographs, teardown images, lot-specific reports, or unverified engineering measurements as actual project evidence.

14. Future Catalog Expansion

Add:

Manufacturer-specific product indices.

Compatible controller matrices.
CAD and drawing availability indices.
Joint-specific shortlist records.
Sample history.
Pilot-order history.

All product information remains subject to source verification and revision control.
