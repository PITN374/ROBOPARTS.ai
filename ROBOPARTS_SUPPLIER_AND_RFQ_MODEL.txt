1. Purpose

Establish the commercial intelligence and sourcing framework connecting component requirements, manufacturers, distributors, engineering documentation, supplier qualification, RFQs, quotations, and procurement decisions.

Supplier type must be supported by evidence where represented as verified.

7. Supplier Matching Model

Proposed configurable score: 0–100.

Criterion	Maximum points
Component relevance	30
Manufacturing capability fit	20
Robotics industry relevance	15
Evidence quality	15
Geographic and logistics fit	10
Business contact quality	10
Total	100

Initial interpretation:

80–100: High-priority candidate.
60–79: Relevant candidate.
40–59: Additional research required.
0–39: Low priority or insufficient evidence.

These weights are proposed and not validated commercial predictions.

Every score must include:

Individual criterion scores.
Supporting evidence references.
Missing information.
Scoring configuration version.
Calculation timestamp.

A high score does not authorize outreach or purchasing.

Missing information must not automatically be interpreted as negative information.

8. Supplier Research Workflow
Define required component family.
Define target specifications and manufacturing processes.
Discover potential companies.
Identify original source records.
Classify supplier type.
Resolve duplicate company identities.
Identify public business contact channels.
Assess evidence quality.
Evaluate manufacturing relevance.
Calculate explainable matching score.
Produce a supplier shortlist.
Request additional evidence where required.
9. RFQ Classification

Proposed RFQ classes:

RFQ-01 — Standard Catalog Product

Used when a commercially available product appears suitable without significant interface changes.

RFQ-02 — Standard Product with Configuration

Used when standard hardware requires a defined choice of encoder, brake, driver, harness, voltage, or protocol.

RFQ-03 — Modified Interface

Used when flange, connector, cable exit, shaft, mounting, or other defined interfaces require modification.

RFQ-04 — OEM Custom Engineering

Used when a supplier must develop a modified or new product architecture.

RFQ-05 — Manufacturing and Component Supply

Used for standalone motors, reducers, structural parts, harnesses, electronics, and other manufactured components.

RFQ-06 — Integrated Actuator System

Used when the buyer requires an integrated mechanical, electrical, feedback, and control package.

RFQ-07 — Linear Motion System

Used for force, stroke, speed, stiffness, and positioning requirements.

RFQ-08 — Controller and Electronics

Used for drivers, motor controllers, communications hardware, and associated firmware.

These classes are proposed internal classifications and may be revised as the catalog develops.

14. Procurement Authorization

The software may prepare RFQs and compare offers.

Separate authorization is required for:

Sending supplier communications.
Accepting offers.
Issuing purchase orders.
Committing funds.
Changing approved specifications.

External communications must use authorized providers and applicable compliance controls.

Dry-run mode is the default.

Suppression records and opt-out instructions must be enforced.
