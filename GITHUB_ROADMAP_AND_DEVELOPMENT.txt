1. PURPOSE

This document establishes the proposed development roadmap for RoboParts.ai.

The objective is to organize development into manageable stages while preserving the broader PITN.ai robotics ecosystem architecture.

RoboParts.ai is intended to evolve beyond a conventional robotics parts directory.

Its proposed long-term role is to provide:

Structured robotics component information.
Product and manufacturer indexing.
Serialized component identity.
Robot-specific bills of materials.
Hardware compatibility information.
Component accountability.
Lifecycle and maintenance tracking.
Recall identification.
Capability relationships.
Digital twin information.
Integration with RoboTraits and RoboAgentic.

Development should proceed incrementally.

2. PROJECT VISION

RoboParts.ai provides the physical hardware information layer for the PITN.ai robotics ecosystem.

The long-term conceptual relationship is:

ROBOT | v CAPABILITIES | v REQUIRED HARDWARE | v ROBOPARTS.AI | v COMPONENTS | v MANUFACTURERS | v SUPPLIERS | v INTEGRATORS | v MAINTENANCE | v LIFECYCLE ACCOUNTABILITY

The platform should connect individual component information with the complete robotic system.

7. PHASE 4 — SUPPLIER AND SAMPLE QUALIFICATION

Objective:

Create a repeatable process connecting engineering requirements to sourcing decisions.

Five-stage workflow:

FIT BASELINE

Confirm joint position, robot class, mechanical envelope, mounting interface, cable exit, and quantity.

DUTY AND THERMAL CHECK

Confirm continuous output, peak output, peak duration, speed, cycle profile, current limit, and ambient conditions.

INTERFACE AND CONTROL CHECK

Confirm voltage, communication protocol, controller, encoder, brake, pinout, firmware, and harness.

SAMPLE VALIDATION

Define pass/fail criteria for backlash, thermal rise, noise, force response, impact, or stroke repeatability.

PILOT CONTINUITY

Define pilot quantity, repeat-order expectations, inspection, packaging, delivery terms, and revision control.

Acceptance criteria:

An accepted sample must have a traceable model, revision, test record, and defined acceptance outcome.

19. IMPLEMENTATION STATUS POLICY

Every major feature should be assigned a status.

PROPOSED: Architectural concept or intended functionality.

IN DEVELOPMENT: Implementation work has started.

IMPLEMENTED: Code or functionality has been completed.

TESTED: Defined tests have been executed successfully.

VERIFIED: Required verification evidence exists.

DEPLOYED: Functionality has been released into an identified operating environment.

These labels must not be used interchangeably.
