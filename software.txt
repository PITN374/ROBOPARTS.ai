EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The PITN.ai ecosystem is a proposed modular software infrastructure for robotics component intelligence, structured robot capabilities, configurable behavioral intelligence, intelligent-agent orchestration, supplier discovery, and governed interaction with robotic systems.

The ecosystem consists of four complementary layers:

PITN.ai The overarching ecosystem, knowledge infrastructure, and integration strategy.

ROBOPARTS.ai The component identity, sourcing, supplier intelligence, evidence provenance, procurement workflow, and lifecycle traceability layer.

ROBOTRAITS.ai / ROBOTRAITS.com The structured robotics ontology and profile layer describing physical capabilities, operating constraints, behavioral traits, personality configurations, performance characteristics, interfaces, and verification status.

ROBOAGENTIC.ai The intelligent-agent implementation and orchestration layer supporting robotics supplier research, multilingual discovery, commercial matching, RFQ preparation, AI-assisted reasoning, context-aware behavior, memory integration, authorized workflow execution, and robot-platform integration.

The intended architecture connects physical-component information, robot configuration data, structured capabilities, behavioral profiles, AI reasoning, and controlled software-agent actions through defined interfaces.

The fundamental principle is that identifying a component, understanding its capabilities, determining its suitability, and authorizing an action are separate technical responsibilities.

The software must preserve these distinctions throughout data processing, reasoning, procurement, and robotic interaction.

This specification defines the intended architecture and proposed development requirements. It does not assert that every described module, API, integration, or capability has already been implemented or deployed.

All material implementation claims must be supported by source code, reproducible tests, running services, or other appropriate evidence.

MISSION AND OBJECTIVES
The mission is to establish a modular, interoperable, evidence-driven software foundation connecting robotics components, robot descriptions, intelligent agents, suppliers, AI services, and authorized robotic platforms.

Primary objectives:

Establish persistent identities for commercial products, physical components, robots, software agents, and relevant digital representations.
Connect component and robot attributes to source evidence, verification status, configuration history, and relevant constraints.
Define a common, versioned representation of robot capabilities and behavioral characteristics through ROBOTRAITS.
Implement ROBOAGENTIC services that interpret structured profiles and coordinate AI reasoning, planning, memory, interaction, and authorized workflows.
Develop supplier discovery, component classification, contact research, commercial matching, and RFQ automation for ROBOPARTS.ai.
Establish centralized authorization, auditable decisions, revocation, and policy enforcement.
Support replaceable AI providers, external data sources, and compatible robot platforms through modular adapters.
Demonstrate technical feasibility through reproducible software tests and a complete end-to-end reference workflow.
Establish an implementation roadmap that progresses from software-only prototypes to selected external integrations and, where appropriate, validated physical-robot deployments.
Provide a foundation for future collaboration among manufacturers, suppliers, system integrators, AI providers, researchers, application developers, and robotics operators.

CORE ECOSYSTEM MODEL
3.1 PITN.ai — Ecosystem and Infrastructure

PITN.ai provides the proposed overarching framework for coordinating the ecosystem's identity, knowledge, data, integration, and development strategy.

Potential responsibilities include:

Common terminology and interoperability principles.
Shared identity and provenance conventions.
Integration standards and architectural governance.
Research coordination and reproducible technical documentation.
Cross-project data and service relationships.
Future knowledge-sharing and infrastructure services.
Any decentralized infrastructure, blockchain integration, governance platform, or shared knowledge network remains subject to explicit design, implementation, and validation.

3.2 ROBOPARTS.ai — Component and Supplier Intelligence

ROBOPARTS.ai establishes the component and commercial information layer.

Its proposed responsibilities include:

Manufacturer and distributor discovery.
Component classification and product identification.
Persistent digital identities and external identifier resolution.
Physical-instance and assembly relationships.
Supplier records and evidence-linked business information.
Request-for-quotation workflows.
Supplier-offer collection and comparison.
Component sourcing and commercial matching.
Installation, maintenance, replacement, and lifecycle records.
Integration with inventory, ERP, PLM, MES, and maintenance systems where authorized.
ROBOPARTS.ai must distinguish a commercial product definition from an individual physical component.

A manufacturer's part number identifies a product or product variant; a serial number may identify an individual unit; a supplier SKU may identify a commercial listing. These identifiers must not be treated as interchangeable.

3.3 ROBOTRAITS.ai — Structured Robotics Ontology

ROBOTRAITS provides a machine-readable description of robots, components, software agents, capabilities, constraints, behavioral tendencies, and performance.

Its proposed responsibilities include:

Physical capability and limitation descriptions.
Sensor and actuator profiles.
Interfaces, dependencies, and operating requirements.
Personality archetypes and configurable behavioral traits.
Communication and interaction profiles.
Cognition-related behavioral preferences.
Performance metrics and test conditions.
Context and memory configuration references.
Lifecycle and configuration relationships.
Evidence and verification status.
Versioned schemas and profile validation.
ROBOTRAITS is a descriptive and evaluative layer. A declared capability does not prove that a physical robot can safely perform a task.

3.4 ROBOAGENTIC.ai — Intelligent-Agent Implementation

ROBOAGENTIC is the proposed intelligent-agent implementation and orchestration layer.

It supports two related agent domains.

A. Robotics intelligence agents:

Interpret ROBOTRAITS profiles.
Coordinate AI reasoning and planning.
Apply configurable behavioral preferences.
Retrieve authorized context and memory.
Select appropriate high-level behaviors.
Match robot capabilities to task requirements.
Communicate through approved interfaces.
Interact with simulated or physical robot platforms through authorized adapters.
B. Robotics sourcing and commercial intelligence agents:

Discover robotics companies and suppliers.
Research publicly documented business information.
Classify component specialities and manufacturing capabilities.
Evaluate commercial relevance to ROBOPARTS.ai.
Identify and verify public business contact channels.
Prepare RFQs and normalize supplier quotations.
Produce operational and sourcing reports.
Both domains may share infrastructure for identity, configuration, evidence, scheduling, model-provider integration, logging, policy enforcement, and reporting.

However, supplier-research agents and physical-robot interaction agents have different permissions, operating environments, risk profiles, and acceptance criteria. They must remain logically separated even when implemented within the same software platform.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The proposed architecture consists of modular services with explicit interfaces.

Core layers:

Data and identity.
Evidence and provenance.
Component and supplier intelligence.
ROBOTRAITS ontology and profile management.
AI reasoning and context management.
Agent registry and orchestration.
Workflow and task execution.
Authorization and policy enforcement.
Robot-platform integration.
Audit, testing, monitoring, and reporting.
Conceptual data flow:

External Sources and Existing Systems -> Source Records and Evidence -> ROBOPARTS Identity and Relationship Resolution -> Component and Robot Configuration -> ROBOTRAITS Capability and Behavioral Profiles -> ROBOAGENTIC Agent Registry and Context -> AI Reasoning and Planning -> Policy and Authorization Evaluation -> Authorized Information Retrieval or Workflow Action -> Audit Record, Result, and Feedback

For physical robot integration, a separate path applies:

ROBOTRAITS Profile -> ROBOAGENTIC Behavior Selection -> Task and Constraint Evaluation -> Authorization -> Universal Robot Interface -> Robot-Specific Adapter -> Approved Middleware or Manufacturer SDK -> Simulated or Physical Robot

The actual implementation must define the inputs, outputs, error handling, trust boundaries, and validation rules for every interface.

No component should receive unrestricted authority merely because it participates in the architecture.

ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES
5.1 Persistent Identity

Digital entities must have stable identifiers independent of changing ownership, location, configuration, or operational state.

5.2 Separation of Concerns

Product definitions, physical instances, assembly positions, robot configurations, traits, memory, personality profiles, permissions, and lifecycle events are distinct entities.

5.3 Evidence-Linked Data

Material claims should reference their sources and verification status.

5.4 Non-Destructive History

Corrections, replacements, revocations, and configuration changes must preserve the history needed to reconstruct earlier states.

5.5 Interoperability

Existing systems remain authoritative for the records they manage unless an explicit integration agreement defines another arrangement.

5.6 Replaceable Providers

AI models, translation services, search providers, databases, email services, and robot adapters should be replaceable through documented interfaces where practical.

5.7 Explicit Authorization

Declared capabilities are not permission grants. Missing, invalid, expired, or revoked authorization must not be interpreted as approval.

5.8 Independent Safety

Personality, AI-generated plans, learned behavior, and ordinary application logic must not override applicable physical safety constraints.

5.9 Implementation Transparency

Proposed, partially implemented, implemented, tested, and independently verified capabilities must be reported separately.

5.10 Reproducibility

Core workflows should be demonstrable using version-controlled source code, controlled test data, documented setup instructions, and repeatable acceptance tests.

ROBOPARTS.AI — COMPONENT IDENTITY AND DATA MODEL
6.1 Proposed Core Entities

Organization Manufacturer, distributor, OEM, system integrator, operator, service provider, or other relevant organization.

ComponentClass A classification such as actuator, motor, sensor, controller, end effector, battery, or structural component.

Product A commercial product definition with manufacturer, part number, model, revision, and applicable specifications.

PhysicalInstance An individual component or production unit with its own identity, serial number where available, lot reference where applicable, and lifecycle history.

Robot A persistent identity for a robotic system.

AssemblyPosition A defined position or role within a robot or assembly.

RobotConfiguration A versioned record of the relevant component relationships and software or hardware revisions.

SourceRecordReference A reference to an external record, such as a manufacturer catalog, supplier listing, ERP record, or maintenance system.

EvidenceReference A reference to documentation, inspection results, measurements, or other supporting evidence.

ComponentRelationship A typed relationship between identified entities.

LifecycleEvent An event such as manufacture, inspection, installation, maintenance, removal, or replacement.

RFQ A request for a component or defined technical requirement.

SupplierOffer A supplier response containing technical and commercial information.

6.2 Component Identity Example

Illustrative JSON structure:

{ "schema_version": "1.0", "component_id": "rp-component-example-001", "component_class": "actuator", "product": { "manufacturer": "Example Manufacturer", "part_number": "EX-ACT-100", "model": "EX-ACT-100", "revision": "A" }, "physical_instance": { "instance_id": "rp-instance-example-001", "serial_number": "EXAMPLE-SERIAL-001", "lot_id": null }, "attributes": { "rated_torque_nm": { "value": null, "unit": "N.m", "status": "unverified", "evidence_refs": [] } }, "lifecycle_status": "unverified", "evidence_refs": [] }

This is an illustrative schema, not a verified production record.

The system must not fabricate specifications or fill missing attributes with invented values.

6.3 Identifier Resolution

The identity service should:

Preserve original identifiers and their issuing systems.
Support multiple identifiers for the same entity.
Distinguish product, serial, lot, supplier, and internal identifiers.
Normalize identifiers without destroying their original representations.
Detect collisions, ambiguity, and conflicting mappings.
Record the evidence and method supporting identity resolution.
Avoid silently merging entities when identity is uncertain.
Preserve removed components and their lifecycle records.
A namespace such as .Actuator may serve as a classification or resolution concept. Global uniqueness, registration, ownership, and technical availability must be established independently rather than assumed from the namespace label.

6.4 Lifecycle and Configuration History

Candidate lifecycle events include:

component.manufacture component.inspection component.shipment component.receipt component.installation component.calibration component.maintenance component.removal component.replacement component.transfer component.decommission

Relevant event fields should include:

event_id event_type subject_id related_entity_ids valid_time recorded_time source_record_refs evidence_refs validation_status actor_or_organization, where established event_metadata

The implementation should distinguish when an event occurred from when it was recorded.

Replacing an actuator must create or associate a new physical-instance identity and update the relevant robot configuration without erasing the removed component's history.

7.6 Commercial Matching

The proposed configurable 0–100 relevance score may use these initial weights:

Component relevance: 0–30 points. Manufacturing capability fit: 0–20 points. Robotics industry relevance: 0–15 points. Evidence quality: 0–15 points. Geographic and logistics fit: 0–10 points. Business contact quality: 0–10 points.

Suggested initial interpretation:

80–100: High-priority candidate. 60–79: Relevant candidate. 40–59: Additional research needed. 0–39: Low priority or insufficient evidence.

These weights are configuration proposals, not validated commercial predictions.

Every score should include an explanation and supporting evidence references.

Missing information must not automatically be treated as negative information. A high score must never independently authorize outreach, procurement, or another consequential action.

ROBOTRAITS.AI — ONTOLOGY AND PROFILE MODEL
ROBOTRAITS defines the common representation for robot and component characteristics, behavioral tendencies, capabilities, constraints, interfaces, and supporting evidence.

9.1 Core Profile Categories

Identity: Persistent identifiers, manufacturer, model, revision, ownership, provenance, and relationships.

Physical: Mobility, manipulation, payload, balance, range, mass, dimensions, and physical limitations.

Sensors: Cameras, microphones, LiDAR, tactile sensors, force sensors, proximity sensors, IMUs, and environmental sensors.

Actuators: Motors, joints, servos, hands, grippers, wheels, and end effectors.

Capabilities: Conversation, perception, recognition, reasoning, planning, learning, navigation, manipulation, and supported tasks.

Personality: Configurable behavioral dimensions and archetypes.

Behavior: Observable actions, decision patterns, and context-dependent behavior-selection preferences.

Interaction: Voice, speech rate, tone, gestures, turn-taking, response length, and communication style.

Cognition: Planning depth, verification tendencies, risk sensitivity, persistence, and uncertainty handling.

Memory: Context references, conversation history, episodic memories, semantic knowledge, and task history, subject to access controls.

User Preferences: Permitted personalization settings, interaction preferences, and authorized adaptations.

Safety: Permissions, operating limits, restricted actions, human override, and relevant safety-system references.

Interfaces: ROS, manufacturer SDKs, APIs, WebSocket, gRPC, programming languages, and simulation interfaces.

Performance: Accuracy, precision, latency, reliability, speed, payload, task success, battery life, and operating range.

Digital Twin: Associated representation of identity, configuration, state, performance, and maintenance.

Lifecycle: Configuration, training, deployment, maintenance, updates, service history, and retirement.

Provenance: Sources, issuers, timestamps, test conditions, verification methods, and relevant evidence.

9.2 Machine-Readable Trait Schema

A trait object should support:

trait_id trait_name trait_category definition measurement_method value unit, where applicable confidence, where supported context activation_conditions behavioral_effects constraints version provenance

The implementation must define field types, units, permitted values, validation rules, and required versus optional attributes.

Manufacturer-specific extensions should not break the common schema.

9.3 Evidence and Verification Status

Attributes should distinguish applicable statuses such as:

declared inferred verified disputed unverified

These statuses are not interchangeable confidence scores.

A profile should record who or what supplied an attribute, how it was evaluated, the scope of verification, relevant evidence, and any expiration or invalidation conditions.

A manufacturer-declared torque rating is not equivalent to an independently measured result.

A documented compatibility claim is not equivalent to verified compatibility with a particular robot configuration.

9.4 Physical Capabilities and Personality Are Separate

One physical robot may support multiple ROBOTRAITS behavioral profiles.

Its physical identity and actual hardware capabilities must remain separate from personality configuration, user preferences, memory, and interaction style.

Changing personality must not change the recorded physical limits or grant new physical capabilities.

Analytical Researcher.
Creative Innovator.
Human-Centered Visionary.
Creative Individualist.
Collaborative Mentor.
Enthusiastic Explorer.
Precision Guardian.

Operational Organizer.
Community Coordinator.

10.3 Trait Effects

Each implemented trait should specify its intended behavioral effects and how those effects are evaluated.

Examples:

High patience may reduce interruptions and allow more time for user responses.

High curiosity may encourage appropriate questions and exploration within permitted task boundaries.

High verification may increase the use of evidence checks before responding or acting.

High task orientation may prioritize the assigned objective within the approved policy.

High caution may favor lower-risk choices when multiple authorized alternatives are available.

These effects are design hypotheses until demonstrated through testing.

10.4 Personality Proof of Concept

The initial experiment should determine whether different ROBOTRAITS profiles produce measurably different and consistent behavior.

Protocol:

Define a versioned trait schema and two meaningfully different profiles.
Use the same AI model, robot or simulator, task, and baseline configuration.
Control other relevant variables.
Define observable behavioral metrics.
Repeat trials.
Measure response style, interruptions, verification, task success, consistency, and safety compliance.
Document results, variability, failures, and limitations.
Personality is a configurable behavioral model, not a claim about consciousness or human-equivalent psychology.

ROBOAGENTIC.AI — INTELLIGENT-AGENT ARCHITECTURE
11.1 Agent Responsibilities

The platform may implement specialized agents with defined inputs, outputs, permissions, and responsibilities.

Discovery Agent: Finds candidate companies and preserves source evidence.

Contact Agent: Identifies public business contact channels and records provenance.

Classification Agent: Classifies companies, components, materials, manufacturing processes, and robotics categories.

Verification Agent: Evaluates source quality, identifies conflicts, and checks verification status.

Matching Agent: Scores commercial relevance and explains supporting evidence.

RFQ Agent: Prepares structured RFQs from approved requirements and bills of materials.

Robot-Profile Agent: Retrieves and interprets authorized ROBOTRAITS profiles.

Reasoning and Planning Agent: Coordinates supported AI reasoning and task planning.

Memory and Context Agent: Retrieves or updates authorized context and memory.

Interaction Agent: Manages permitted dialogue, communication style, and personalization.

Robot-Integration Agent: Translates supported high-level actions through approved robot interfaces.

Policy Agent: Evaluates permissions, source restrictions, outreach eligibility, and action constraints.

Reporting Agent: Produces operational reports, metrics, errors, and evidence summaries.

These are logical responsibilities. They may be implemented as separate services, modules, or coordinated processes according to the chosen architecture.

No agent may bypass centralized policy enforcement, suppression controls, credential checks, or robot-specific safety boundaries.

11.2 Core Agent Model

A ROBOAGENTIC profile may contain:

agent_id identity owner_or_responsible_organization software_version interface_versions declared_capabilities dependencies available_tools data_sources requested_permissions approved_permissions policy_version credential_status expiration registration_history revocation_history audit_references

A declared capability does not grant authorization.

11.3 Behavior Model

A conceptual behavior model is:

BEHAVIOR = f( PERSONALITY, MEMORY, CONTEXT, TASK, ENVIRONMENT, AI MODEL, CURRENT STATE, SAFETY CONSTRAINTS )

The model describes relevant inputs, not a claim that the resulting behavior is fully predictable.

The system should distinguish:

Core behavioral configuration.
Task-specific instructions.
Current contextual information.
Persistent authorized memory.
Learned model parameters.
User preferences.
Safety policies and permissions.
These inputs must be governed separately.

11.4 Memory and Personalization

Potential memory types include:

Short-term task context.
Conversation history.
Episodic memory.
Semantic knowledge.
User preferences.
Environmental information.
Task history.
Memory storage and retrieval must be permission-aware and appropriate to the application.

Adaptive loop:

Interaction -> Observation -> Feedback -> Evaluation -> Authorized Preference Update -> Future Behavior

Meaningful changes should be recorded and, where appropriate, reversible.

Users and operators should have suitable controls for reviewing, correcting, and removing stored information.

Learning from interactions must not silently modify safety-critical policies or physical operating limits.

15.2 Robot Adapter Responsibilities

A robot adapter should:

Expose supported operations through a documented interface.
Validate action requests and their parameters.
Map high-level requests to platform-specific commands.
Check relevant configuration and operating constraints.
Enforce applicable authorization requirements.
Report execution status and errors.
Record relevant command and result metadata.

15.3 Physical and Simulated Platforms

A software-only implementation may use simulated robot profiles and mocked actions.

A successful software test does not establish physical compatibility, safe motion, or production readiness.

SAFETY, AUTHORIZATION, AND GOVERNANCE
16.1 Priority Model

Safety and applicable physical limits -> Authorization and mandatory application constraints -> Task requirements -> Behavior selection -> Personality preferences

Personality, memory, model output, and learned behavior must never override applicable safety constraints or permissions.

16.2 Authorization Decisions

The policy service should evaluate:

Requesting agent identity and status.
Requested operation.
Target resource.
Granted permissions.
Credential validity.
Relevant contextual restrictions.
Policy version.
Time limits and expiration.
Required approvals.
Revocation status.
The result must be explicit:

PERMITTED DENIED UNRESOLVED

An unresolved decision must not silently become permission.

16.3 Revocation

The system should demonstrate that revoked credentials or permissions cannot be reused through the tested interfaces.

Relevant considerations include:

Cached credentials.
Active sessions.
Delegated permissions.
Long-running jobs.
Previously issued tokens.
Pending actions.
Revocation behavior must be defined for each integration.

16.4 Emergency Stop and Safe-State Management

The architecture must distinguish:

Permission revocation: Prevents further authorized access.

Software workflow restriction: Prevents specified application actions from proceeding.

Physical emergency stop: Uses appropriate robot-specific safety mechanisms to bring hazardous operation to a safe state.

A software-only demonstration can validate simulated restriction and revocation behavior.

Physical emergency stopping requires appropriate hardware, hazard analysis, independent protective mechanisms, and system-specific validation.

An ordinary API call, blockchain transaction, remote service, AI model, or software flag must not be represented as a certified safety-rated emergency stop.

Removing power indiscriminately may itself create hazards in some systems. The appropriate safe state must be engineered for the target robot.

16.5 Cybersecurity

Security requirements should include:

Authentication and authorization.
Least-privilege access.
Secret management.
Secure communications.
Input validation.
Credential rotation and revocation.
Dependency management.
Appropriate software-update controls.
Logging and monitoring.
Backup and recovery.
Security incident procedures.
Retrieved websites, documents, emails, and model outputs must be treated as untrusted data.

External content must not override system policy or authorize access to credentials.

Responses to inbound inquiries.
Required controls:

Accurate sender identity.
Authorized email provider.
Applicable authentication and domain configuration, including SPF, DKIM, and DMARC where appropriate.
Provider rate and volume limits.
Applicable jurisdiction-specific requirements.
Required business identification and opt-out mechanisms.
Prompt unsubscribe processing.
Centralized suppression records.
Duplicate-send prevention.
Provider message identifiers and delivery events.
Appropriate bounce handling.
Bounded retries for eligible transient errors.
Audit records of sending decisions.
The system must not bypass recipient blocks, evade spam controls, conceal sender identity, or ignore opt-out requests.

Failed delivery must trigger diagnosis and compliant recovery, not evasion.

Dry-run mode should be enabled by default.

Outbound communications must stop safely when authorization, eligibility, or policy status is absent or unclear.

The platform must not claim guaranteed inbox placement or universal supplier coverage.

INTEGRITY, PROVENANCE, AND OPTIONAL BLOCKCHAIN
The evidence model should be implemented before adding optional blockchain infrastructure.

Proposed integrity workflow:

Record or Lifecycle Event -> Canonical Serialization -> Cryptographic Hash -> Optional Tamper-Evident Anchor -> Verification Procedure -> Integrity Result

The implementation must define:

Canonical serialization rules.
Hashing algorithm.
Content covered by the hash.
Record and schema versioning.
Anchor reference format.
Verification procedure.
Failure behavior.
Key and reference management where applicable.
Sensitive supplier information, credentials, personal information, and detailed operational records should remain in appropriately controlled storage.

A successful hash comparison can establish that compared content matches the content represented by the reference hash, subject to the integrity of the hashing and verification process.

It does not establish that a component is authentic, that a physical event occurred, that a capability claim is correct, or that a supplier owns submitted documentation.

Those claims require appropriate evidence and verification procedures.

Blockchain integration should be included only where its benefits justify the added complexity.

TESTING AND ACCEPTANCE REQUIREMENTS
Testing must cover data correctness, interface behavior, authorization, reliability, and evidence integrity.

23.1 Component and Identity Tests

AT-01: Resolve a known manufacturer part number.

Expected result: Correct product and revision, or a documented limitation.

AT-02: Resolve a known physical instance.

Expected result: Correct physical-instance and product relationships.

AT-03: Resolve an ambiguous identifier.

Expected result: Ambiguity is explicitly reported.

AT-04: Retrieve lifecycle events.

Expected result: Expected events and source references are returned.

AT-05: Reconstruct a historical robot configuration.

Expected result: Output matches a predefined reference dataset.

AT-06: Replace a component.

Expected result: Old and new identities and histories are preserved.

23.2 ROBOTRAITS Tests

AT-07: Validate trait schemas.

Expected result: Invalid values and missing required fields are flagged.

AT-08: Process conflicting attributes.

Expected result: Conflicting claims remain distinguishable and traceable.

AT-09: Compare capability profiles against task requirements.

Expected result: Matching criteria, missing information, and constraints are reported.

AT-10: Change a component configuration.

Expected result: Applicable capability assessments are reassessed.

AT-11: Compare personality profiles.

Expected result: Measurable behavioral differences are evaluated under controlled conditions.

23.3 ROBOAGENTIC Tests

AT-12: Register an agent.

Expected result: Identity, version, and declared capabilities are retrievable.

AT-13: Request a permitted operation.

Expected result: Operation is allowed within its defined scope and audited.

AT-14: Request a prohibited operation.

Expected result: Operation is denied and the decision is recorded.

AT-15: Revoke an agent.

Expected result: Subsequent protected requests are denied through the tested interfaces.

AT-16: Request inaccessible evidence.

Expected result: Access is denied or an appropriate unavailable status is returned.

23.4 Supplier Intelligence Tests

AT-17: Extract and normalize company records.

Expected result: Source references are preserved.

AT-18: Deduplicate companies.

Expected result: Duplicates are handled without merging ambiguous identities silently.

AT-19: Calculate matching scores.

Expected result: Scores and explanations match the configured rules.

AT-20: Validate contact provenance.

Expected result: Unsupported or guessed contact details are not marked verified.

AT-21: Normalize supplier quotations.

Expected result: Incomplete or incomparable commercial values are flagged.

23.5 Operational and Security Tests

AT-22: Verify record integrity.

Expected result: Unchanged content verifies; altered content fails verification.

AT-23: Test dry-run outreach.

Expected result: No unintended external message is sent.

AT-24: Test suppression enforcement.

Expected result: Suppressed recipients are not sent messages through the tested workflows.

AT-25: Test duplicate-send prevention.

Expected result: Repeated task execution does not produce duplicate sends.

AT-26: Test rate limits and retries.

Expected result: Defined limits are respected and retries remain bounded.

AT-27: Test service unavailability.

Expected result: Missing dependencies do not silently grant permissions.

AT-28: Test secrets redaction.

Expected result: Credentials are not exposed in logs or reports.

AT-29: Test report accuracy.

Expected result: Reported metrics correspond to recorded events.

AT-30: Test simulated emergency restriction.

Expected result: Protected software actions are blocked as specified.

Every acceptance test must record its initial state, test data, procedure, expected result, observed result, pass/fail status, evidence location, and any associated defect.

Physical safety and hardware compatibility require separate validation when applicable.

FINAL TECHNICAL PROPOSITION
The PITN.ai ecosystem is designed around a progression from component identity and sourcing to structured robotic knowledge and governed intelligent-agent interaction.

ROBOPARTS.ai establishes component and supplier intelligence. It connects commercial product definitions, physical instances, source evidence, assembly relationships, RFQs, and lifecycle records without requiring every participating organization to replace its existing systems.

ROBOTRAITS.ai provides a common language for describing robot and component characteristics, physical capabilities, operational constraints, behavioral tendencies, personality configurations, interfaces, and supporting evidence.

ROBOAGENTIC.ai implements intelligent agents that interpret these profiles, coordinate AI reasoning and planning, manage authorized context and personalization, conduct supplier research, prepare sourcing workflows, and interact with compatible robot platforms through approved interfaces.

PITN.ai provides the overarching ecosystem and integration strategy connecting these complementary layers.

The combined architecture follows this progression:

IDENTIFY THE ENTITY.

ESTABLISH ITS RELATIONSHIPS.

PRESERVE ITS EVIDENCE.

DESCRIBE ITS CAPABILITIES AND CONSTRAINTS.

DEFINE ITS BEHAVIORAL PROFILE WHERE APPLICABLE.

REGISTER THE AGENT.

EVALUATE THE TASK AND CONTEXT.

ENFORCE AUTHORIZATION AND SAFETY REQUIREMENTS.

EXECUTE ONLY APPROVED WORKFLOWS.

RECORD THE RESULT.

VERIFY WHAT THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES.

The objective is to establish a useful, interoperable, auditable, and extensible software foundation for robotics component intelligence, supplier sourcing, structured robot capabilities, configurable behavior, and governed agentic systems.

The first priority is a narrow, reproducible software demonstration. Additional integrations, autonomy, learning, decentralized infrastructure, and physical-robot capabilities should follow only when their requirements, benefits, and validation criteria are established.
