1. PURPOSE

This document defines the proposed digital identity, component identification, bill of materials (BOM), and relationship architecture for RoboParts.ai.

RoboParts.ai is intended to provide a structured physical-hardware information layer for robotic systems.

Its purpose extends beyond a conventional parts directory.

The proposed platform should connect:

Individual physical components.
Manufacturer product families.
Commercial product models.
Product revisions.
Serialized component instances.
Robot assemblies.
Subsystems.
Robot identities.
Capabilities.
Suppliers.
Integrators.
Lifecycle records.
Maintenance and recall information.

The architecture should provide accountability for the physical components assigned to each designated robot.

2. CORE IDENTITY PRINCIPLE

A product model and an individual physical component are not the same thing.

For example:

Manufacturer: Example Manufacturer

Product Family: Integrated Rotary Actuator

Model: Example-01

Revision: B

Individual Component: RP-000001

Serial Number: SN-123456

Robot Assignment: ROBOT-0001

The model identifies a commercial product.

The serial number identifies a particular manufactured unit.

The RoboParts component identifier provides an internal reference to that physical unit within the platform.

These identifiers should remain distinct.

3. PROPOSED IDENTITY HIERARCHY

PITN.ai | v ROBOT IDENTITY | v ROBOT CONFIGURATION | v SUBSYSTEM | v ASSEMBLY | v COMPONENT INSTANCE | v MANUFACTURER PRODUCT | v PRODUCT FAMILY

The system should support relationships in both directions.

A component should be traceable to the robot in which it is installed.

A robot should be traceable to the components installed within it.

4. COMPONENT IDENTITY

Every registered component should receive a stable internal identifier.

Example:

Component ID: RP-000001

Suggested fields:

component_id
component_type
component_class
product_id
manufacturer_id
manufacturer_part_number
manufacturer_serial_number
product_revision
manufacturing_lot
manufacturing_date
received_date
ownership_reference
supplier_id
verification_status
lifecycle_status

An internal identifier must not be confused with a manufacturer serial number.

If the manufacturer does not provide a serial number, the absence must be recorded rather than inventing one.

5. PRODUCT AND COMPONENT DISTINCTION

The architecture should distinguish the following entities:

5.1 Product Family

A collection of related products sharing a general architecture.

Example:

Integrated Rotary Actuators.

5.2 Product Model

A specific manufacturer model.

Example:

Manufacturer Model X.

5.3 Product Revision

A documented revision or variant of a product.

5.4 Commercial Product

The product offered for sale or sourcing.

5.5 Physical Component Instance

The actual unit received, installed, tested, repaired, or retired.

5.6 Custom OEM Variant

A product modified or manufactured to customer-specific requirements.

OEM modifications must be separately identified and revision-controlled.

6. ROBOT IDENTITY

Every registered robot should have a stable internal identifier.

Example:

Robot ID: ROBOT-0001

Suggested fields:

robot_id
robot_name
robot_class
platform_model
manufacturer
owner_reference
integrator_id
configuration_id
robot_revision
deployment_status
commissioning_date
retirement_date
digital_twin_reference
created_at
updated_at

Robot identity must remain stable when individual components are replaced.

Replacing a motor must not create a new robot identity.

7. SUBSYSTEM AND ASSEMBLY STRUCTURE

The system should support hierarchical relationships.

Example:

HUMANOID ROBOT | +-- LEFT ARM | | | +-- SHOULDER ASSEMBLY | +-- ELBOW ASSEMBLY | +-- WRIST ASSEMBLY | +-- RIGHT ARM | | | +-- SHOULDER ASSEMBLY | +-- ELBOW ASSEMBLY | +-- WRIST ASSEMBLY | +-- LEFT LEG | +-- RIGHT LEG | +-- TORSO | +-- HEAD

Each subsystem may contain assemblies, components, and subcomponents.

The architecture must support mirrored left/right assemblies without assuming that their components are identical.

Accessories must not automatically be treated as included in an actuator or product package.

11. COMPONENT-TO-CAPABILITY RELATIONSHIPS

A component may contribute one or more capabilities.

Examples:

Sensor → Perception

Actuator → Movement

Robotic Hand → Manipulation

Battery → Energy Availability

Controller → Motion Control

The relationship should be recorded independently of the component's physical identity.

A proposed capability record may contain:

capability_id
capability_name
description
required_components
applicable_robot_classes
verification_status
evidence_reference

A robot's available capabilities should be derived from its actual configuration and operational state.

12. DIGITAL IDENTITY AND ROBOAGENTIC

The broader PITN.ai ecosystem may allow RoboAgentic.ai to act as an intelligent information and oversight layer for registered components.

Potential responsibilities include:

Summarizing component identity.
Reporting current configuration.
Identifying missing documentation.
Highlighting compatibility concerns.
Summarizing lifecycle history.
Identifying relevant recalls.
Reporting component performance.
Providing authorized maintenance recommendations.

An AI-generated assessment must remain distinguishable from manufacturer documentation, verified test results, and formal engineering approval.

RoboAgentic.ai must not be represented as an independent safety authority unless a separately validated authority and authorization architecture is established.

13. ROBO TRAITS INTEGRATION

RoboTraits.ai may use verified hardware information to describe a robot's characteristics and capabilities.

For example:

Installed Robotic Hand | v Verified Manipulation Capability | v Robot Capability Profile | v RoboTraits

A hardware change may require reassessment of the relevant robot characteristics.

RoboTraits information should not replace component identity or manufacturer specifications.

14. DIGITAL TWIN RELATIONSHIP

A digital twin may represent:

Robot identity.
Current BOM.
Component assignments.
Product revisions.
Operating state.
Capability profile.
Configuration history.
Maintenance history.
Fault history.
Performance information.

The digital representation should distinguish current physical state from intended design configuration.

A digital twin must not be assumed to be synchronized with the physical robot without supporting evidence.

15. IDENTITY CHANGE CONTROL

The following should not normally change after assignment:

Internal robot ID.
Internal component ID.
Manufacturer serial number, where provided.
Historical transaction identifiers.

Changes should instead be recorded as new events or revisions.

Examples:

Component replacement.
Robot reassignment.
Subsystem relocation.
Product revision update.
Firmware change.
Configuration update.
Maintenance event.

Historical records must remain attributable to the correct physical unit.

16. DATA INTEGRITY

The system should support:

Unique identifiers.
Referential integrity.
Versioned BOMs.
Auditable assignment history.
Source references.
Evidence references.
Timestamped changes.
Controlled deletion or retirement.
Access control.
Data validation.

A historical component assignment should not be silently overwritten when a replacement occurs.
