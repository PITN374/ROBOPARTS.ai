1. PURPOSE

This document establishes the proposed safety architecture for RoboParts.ai.

The objective is to provide a structured framework for identifying, documenting, monitoring, and managing safety-related characteristics of robotic components and assemblies.

The architecture addresses:

Emergency stop systems.
Safe Torque Off (STO).
Safety-related component identification.
Fault detection and handling.
Hardware operating limits.
Component safety boundaries.
Human override.
Recall and replacement accountability.
Safety-related configuration changes.
Relationships between physical hardware and intelligent-agent systems.

RoboParts.ai is intended to provide the physical hardware identity and accountability layer within the broader PITN.ai robotics ecosystem.

This document defines an architectural framework. It does not certify a robot, establish a safety integrity level, or demonstrate compliance with any particular safety standard.

2. CORE SAFETY PRINCIPLE

Physical safety must remain independent of learned behavior, personality settings, user preferences, and ordinary AI decision-making.

The proposed architecture distinguishes:

Physical safety mechanisms.
Safety-rated control systems.
Ordinary robot control systems.
Intelligent-agent decision systems.
Digital identity and accountability systems.

An intelligent agent must not be treated as a substitute for a validated safety system.

RoboAgentic.ai may provide monitoring, analysis, and authorized control requests, but safety-related functions must be implemented through appropriately designed and validated hardware and control architectures.

3. SAFETY ARCHITECTURE

Proposed relationship:

ROBOT | v PHYSICAL COMPONENTS | v SAFETY-RELATED HARDWARE | v SAFETY CONTROL SYSTEM | +---- Emergency Stop | +---- Safe Torque Off | +---- Protective Devices | +---- Fault Monitoring | v ROBOT CONTROL SYSTEM | v ROBOAGENTIC.AI | v ROBOT BEHAVIOR

RoboParts.ai maintains the identity, configuration, compatibility, and lifecycle information associated with the physical components and safety-related assemblies.

4. EMERGENCY STOP ARCHITECTURE

Emergency stop systems are intended to provide a means of initiating an emergency response when a hazardous condition occurs.

A proposed emergency stop architecture may include:

Physical emergency stop buttons.
Safety-rated input devices.
Safety relays or safety controllers.
Safety-rated drive interfaces.
STO-capable motor drives.
Documented reset procedures.
Emergency stop status monitoring.

An emergency stop must be designed according to the applicable machine and robot safety requirements.

An ordinary software command, application button, network message, or AI instruction must not be represented as an equivalent replacement for a validated emergency stop system.

4.1 Emergency Stop Identification

Each emergency stop device or safety-related assembly should have an identifiable record.

4.2 Emergency Stop Behavior

The intended behavior must be established through a formal risk assessment and validated safety design.

Documentation should identify:

Hazard addressed.
Initiating device.
Safety logic.
Affected motion or energy sources.
Required stopping behavior.
Reset conditions.
Restart conditions.
Validation evidence.

An emergency stop activation must not automatically authorize a subsequent restart.

5. SAFE TORQUE OFF (STO)

Safe Torque Off is a safety-related drive function intended to prevent a motor from producing torque.

STO is not necessarily equivalent to mechanical braking or complete electrical isolation.

Depending on the application, additional measures may be necessary to address:

Gravity-driven movement.
Falling loads.
Stored mechanical energy.
Residual electrical energy.
Pneumatic or hydraulic energy.
Unexpected movement after power restoration.
5.1 STO Identification

Suggested component attributes:

sto_supported
sto_implementation
sto_channel_configuration
sto_certification_reference
drive_model
drive_firmware
safety_controller_reference
safety_validation_status
installation_revision

These fields must be populated from manufacturer documentation and actual system verification.

5.2 STO Verification

STO compatibility must be evaluated for the actual motor drive, wiring, safety controller, firmware, and installation configuration.

A general statement that a motor controller supports STO is insufficient to establish that the complete robot assembly has a validated safety function.

6. PHYSICAL SAFETY BOUNDARIES

Every relevant component should have documented operating limits.

Examples include:

Maximum continuous torque.
Peak torque and permitted duration.
Maximum rotational speed.
Maximum linear force.
Maximum stroke.
Maximum current.
Permitted bus voltage.
Maximum operating temperature.
Permitted duty cycle.
Maximum payload.
Mechanical travel limits.
Environmental limitations.

Safety-related limits must be distinguished from ordinary performance ratings.

A product's peak performance rating must not be treated as an approved continuous operating condition.

7.2 Fault Severity

Proposed classifications:

CRITICAL: Potential immediate safety hazard requiring the appropriate validated protective response.

HIGH: Serious malfunction or component degradation requiring restricted operation or maintenance assessment.

MEDIUM: Abnormal operation requiring investigation.

LOW: Informational or maintenance-related event.

The severity classification must be based on the application-specific risk assessment, not solely on a software-generated label.

8. SAFETY AND INTELLIGENT AGENTS

RoboAgentic.ai may interact with component information and robot operating data.

However:

AI decisions must not bypass safety interlocks.
Learned behavior must remain subject to physical limits.
User preferences must not override safety requirements.
Software updates must not silently disable safety functions.
Component substitutions must undergo compatibility assessment.
Safety-related configuration changes must be controlled.
Safety faults must not be automatically cleared by an ordinary AI agent.

RoboTraits may describe personality and behavioral characteristics.

Those characteristics must not alter the authority or operation of safety-rated control functions.

9. KILL SWITCH AND REMOTE CONTROL DISTINCTION

The term "kill switch" may refer to different functions and must be precisely defined.

The architecture distinguishes:

Physical emergency stop.
Safety-rated STO activation.
Software operational disable.
Remote administrative disable.
Recall-related restriction.
Component quarantine.
Agent authorization revocation.

These functions are not interchangeable.

A recall flag or digital disable command does not physically stop a robot unless an independently validated control architecture implements that behavior.

Remote shutdown or disable functions must not be relied upon as the sole emergency safety mechanism.

10. COMPONENT RECALL AND SAFETY ACCOUNTABILITY

RoboParts.ai should support identification of components affected by:

Manufacturer recalls.
Safety notices.
Defective production lots.
Firmware advisories.
Design revisions.
Unauthorized substitutions.
End-of-life notifications.

The system should be able to identify:

Affected manufacturer.
Model and revision.
Serial numbers.
Production lots.
Robots containing affected components.
Relevant subsystems.
Installation dates.
Replacement history.
Inspection status.

A recall record should distinguish between a warning, an inspection requirement, a usage restriction, and a confirmed safety-critical defect.

Mechanical redesign.
Safety controller changes.
Emergency stop circuit changes.

Each change should have:

Previous configuration.
Proposed configuration.
Engineering review.
Risk assessment reference.
Test requirements.
Approval record.
Verification evidence.
Release authorization.

14. ENGINEERING AND COMPLIANCE DISCLAIMER

This architecture is a documentation and information-management framework.

It is not a substitute for:

Formal machinery risk assessment.
Functional safety engineering.
Certified safety components.
Validated safety circuits.
Applicable robot safety standards.
Qualified engineering review.
System-level testing and validation.

Applicable requirements may include ISO 10218, ISO 12100, ISO 13849-1, IEC 62061, and IEC 61800-5-2, depending on the machine, application, jurisdiction, and design.

The applicability and current editions of standards must be independently confirmed.
