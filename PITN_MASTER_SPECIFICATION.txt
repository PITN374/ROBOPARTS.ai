ONTOLOGY

ROBOTRAITS provides a shared semantic foundation for robotics component classifications, specifications, capabilities, performance and compatibility.

UHMO / UHO represents a proposed specialized ontology for humanoid manipulation.

Every product, organization, component and digital asset must have a persistent identifier.

Specifications must include appropriate data types, units and provenance.

Relationships must define their meanings and valid source and target types.

Records should track ownership, verification status and version history.

RoboHands Dexterous robotic hands and manipulation systems.

RoboEffectors Grippers, tools and specialized end effectors.

RoboJoints Actuated joints, rotary modules and precision drives.

RoboModules Integrated motion subsystems and modular assemblies.

ROBOPARTS.ai Component discovery, spare parts, replacement parts, supplier listings and lifecycle relationships.

DEVELOPMENT PHASES

Phase 1: Product database, taxonomy, manufacturer profiles, structured specifications, search and administration.

Phase 2: Supplier network, RFQs, quotations, supplier matching and membership functionality.

Phase 3: Lifecycle traceability, maintenance, repair, refurbishment and component substitution.

Phase 4: Knowledge graph, AI-assisted discovery, compatibility recommendations, predictive maintenance and APIs.

ADAPTIVE ROBOTICS

Represent software updates, AI updates, configuration changes, user preferences, task experience, environmental changes, hardware upgrades, component replacement and maintenance.

Adaptive loop:

Task Observation Decision Action Result Feedback Evaluation Adjustment

Learning records must remain distinct from persistent component identity records.

HARDWARE AND CAPABILITIES

Hardware changes may affect available capabilities.

A new sensor may improve perception.

A new robotic hand may enable additional manipulation tasks.

A component replacement may require compatibility assessment and renewed testing.

Declared capabilities must remain distinct from verified capabilities.

SAFETY

Represent force, speed, payload, joint, temperature and operating-environment constraints.

Behavioral preferences and AI recommendations must not override safety controls, physical constraints, authorization requirements or emergency procedures.

KNOWLEDGE GRAPH

Connect manufacturers, products, components, specifications, capabilities, robots, BOM positions, suppliers, lifecycle events, evidence and performance records.

Start with relational database relationships. Introduce a graph database when justified by requirements.

AI INTELLIGENCE

Support natural-language product search, specification comparison, supplier discovery, missing-information detection, compatibility analysis and lifecycle summarization.

Distinguish verified specifications, supplier-declared information, estimates and missing data.

DATA GOVERNANCE

Persistent identifiers Duplicate detection Product revision history Source attribution Verification workflows Access permissions Audit history Ontology versioning Controlled corrections

VALIDATION

Measure component identification time, manual reconciliation effort, specification completeness, compatibility accuracy, supplier discovery time and traceability reconstruction time.

Any efficiency improvement is a hypothesis until measured against a documented baseline.

IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLE

Build the technical database and supplier discovery workflow first.

Expand incrementally into lifecycle traceability, ontology relationships, marketplace functionality and AI intelligence.
